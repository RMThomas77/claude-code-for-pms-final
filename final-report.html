Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 · Product School lab (fictional scenario)

We shortened a timer, and four responders went silent.

Release 4.2 made two routing changes on 12 August. Together with an old scoring rule, they quietly stopped Dispatch from asking some perfectly willing responders to attend callouts. This is what happened, what the evidence says, the fix I'd propose, and the habit that keeps a release like this from shipping unchecked again.

1 · Plainly

What happened with 4.2

Dispatch ranks every available responder and asks them one at a time. A responder's rank depends on a score that goes up when they take a ping and down when they turn one down or miss one. Nothing else puts it back.

What 4.2 changed

90s wait60smore pings count as missed
0.45 distance0.60closeness matters more in the ranking
0.40 history0.25acceptance history matters less

What that did, in order

  1. A shorter wait means more misses, and a miss lowers a score like a refusal.
  2. The heavier distance weight pushes the affected responders down the list.
  3. Lower on the list means they are asked only after others say no. Few pings.
  4. Only a taken ping raises a score, so with no pings there is no way back.

Honest statusThis is the working hypothesis, strongly supported but not proven. Nothing in the data separates the wait from the weights, and there is no answer-time or travel-time field. Engineering needs to confirm it.

3 · The investigation, module by module

One finding and the prompt that produced it

1
Origin Story · get oriented, test the “it's seasonal” theory

Acceptance fell and only partly recovered

Can you pull the September data to see if volume has resolved?
Weekly acceptance was 75–78% before 4.2, fell to 54%, and was back to 73% by the week of 31 Aug. But missed pings jumped from 2–6 a week to 38, 28, 24, 21, and tickets from 5–8 to 20, 27, 32, 25. Partly recovering, not fixed, and seasonality alone doesn't explain the missed-ping jump.
Tested Priya's untested view that this was “mostly seasonal”.
2
Super Hearing · interviews versus tickets

The people complaining weren't the people interviewed

Filter out the background noise (tickets that are resolved, or consistent across versions), and compare to the interviews, what key findings does the combined information tell us?
Of 147 tickets, 45 are “gone before they could answer” (15) or “few/no pings” (30). All open, none before 4.2. The 30 come from four handlers, none of whom were interviewed. Three of four interviewees mentioned callouts gone before answering, but Aunt Dot, Kip and Halloran filed no tickets, so the queue misses them.
Two piles of feedback that didn't agree.
3
Rewind · the numbers behind one responder

It's routing, not demand

Is there any change in callout areas or other data that could impact the numbers for Vesper or others?
Before 4.2 each responder was pinged on every callout in their own area. After, four stopped being pinged for their own areas: Vesper 13 of 41 Old Town callouts, first ping on only 8, down from 58 of 69. Nightwell, Captain Vantage and The Longcast took over. Callouts fell about 14% overall. The four share no area, so shared geography is ruled out.
before: 3% missed
after: ~49% missed
The four responders' missed-ping share. The other twelve rose too, from 2% to 13%, so something global hit everyone and the four are the outliers.
4
X-Ray Vision · reading the routing code

There is no road back

Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
Everyone starts at 0.5. Only one thing ever raises a score, and a miss is treated as a refusal:
+0.08ping taken
−0.12turned down
−0.12missed
Nothing lets a score drift back; an open 2019 TODO asked whether it should. A responder who isn't asked can't earn credit, so once pushed down, they stay down.

5
Super Speed · the brief and the prototype

Recovery has to be fast, and sunk responders need a reset

I think we need the recovery time to be quicker, a way to reset to neutral by responder, and keep the two fixes.
At +0.05 a week it takes about 8 weeks to get Vesper back to neutral (0.5); at +0.15 about 3; at +0.25 about 2. A per-responder reset puts Vesper back in 3rd straight away. Both are written into 05-super-speed/brief.md and tested in prototype.html (next section).
Proximity and starting scores in the prototype are my assumptions; the outcome mixes are real.
6
Sidekicks · turn the habit into a skill

My review habit is now reusable

I want to save a habit as something I can reuse. When I review a brief before it goes any further, here's what I actually check for: - It names who owns it - It says how we'll know it worked - The scope at the end matches the scope at the start - It explains the problem before it proposes a fix - It poses any questions that may not have been answered Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again.
A project skill, .claude/skills/review-checklist/SKILL.md. I then added three more checks (how long the issue has been present, priority, how it became known), ran it on my own brief, and set it to run every Monday over a new Briefs/ folder. Details in section 5.
4 · The fix, through the prototype

Two fixes and a reset get Vesper back in the rotation

Don't change a number quietly. Change what the score measures, and give people who are already stuck a way out.

Fix 1

A missed ping stops counting as a refusal

A score should reflect whether someone says no, not whether they were reached in time.

Fix 2

A score eases back when someone wasn't offered work

Toward 0.5 each week. A responder who keeps refusing still stays down. Proposed +0.15 a week.

One-offReset the four to neutral. A per-responder data fix, so Vesper is back in the rotation within a callout or two rather than weeks.

Try it: Vesper over eight weeks

Reading it honestly: the reset puts Vesper 3rd by a thin margin, and by week 2 they slip to 4th because the others keep earning credit and distance carries 0.60 of the score. The fixes stop the fall and restore a way back; they don't promise Vesper work. Real travel times decide whether neutral is enough.

A compact copy of the prototype's model (same rules, +0.08 / −0.12, real outcome mixes). Proximity and starting scores are assumptions, so read it as an illustration of the mechanism, not a forecast.

After the two fixes

  • Possibly put the ping wait back to 90s. A dev should pull real answer times and replay post-4.2 callouts on 90s and the old weights separately.
  • Possibly ease the distance weight back toward 0.45, but only if real travel times show Vesper is far from callouts.
  • A console view that flags who has gone quiet and why, designed with Sofia.
  • Survey the quiet responders, using cover names only (Security Policy 4.1).

Verdict on my own brief: not yet ready

Fails owner and priority; partial on success measure (no metric or date), scope (the Ask holds three things, not two), duration and how it became known. It is also still over one page. The skill only reports and suggests paste-ready wording. It never edits the brief, so those fixes are still mine to apply.

Now a weekly habit

  • Everyone puts briefs in Briefs/ in the repo.
  • Scheduled task review-checklist-weekly reviews anything new or changed each Monday around 09:09 and sends a notification.
  • Runs only while the Claude app is open. First run Monday 12 Oct.
  • Checked a classmate's brief too: it had an owner and measures, but no stated priority.

LimitsThe skill checks the quality of a brief. It can't tell you the routing is wrong, and it only protects releases that go through a brief.
